# Wallets, Custody, and Security

:::info Time estimate
**Reading:** 24 min · **Videos:** 20 min · **Exercise/review:** 10 min · **Total:** about 54 min
:::

## Learning objectives

By the end, you should be able to explain the core mechanism in plain language, identify its main risks, and apply it to a realistic decision.

## Lesson

### The core idea

A wallet creates or uses private keys, derives public addresses, builds transactions, and signs them. Assets remain recorded on the network. Whoever controls the key can normally authorize the assets; there is usually no password reset at the protocol layer.

### How it works

A custodial service controls keys for you. Self-custody gives control and full operational responsibility. Hot wallets are internet-connected and convenient. Hardware wallets isolate signing material, but their screens, supply chain, backups, and user approvals still matter.

### Why it matters

A seed phrase can recreate every derived key. Never type it into a website, cloud note, chat, password field, or unsolicited support tool. Do not photograph it. Make durable offline backups, test the recovery process safely, and ensure heirs can find instructions without exposing the secret prematurely.

### Safety-first takeaway

Before sending: verify asset, network, full destination, amount, and memo/tag; use an allowlist where available; send a small test; confirm receipt; then send the remainder. Address poisoning and clipboard malware make the first/last four characters insufficient for high-value transfers.

## Knowledge check

<details>
<summary>What should you understand before risking funds?</summary>

The transaction path, custody model, fees, failure modes, recovery procedure, and legal/tax recordkeeping—not merely a price prediction.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What is the safest response when you cannot explain a mechanism?</summary>

Pause. Research it with primary sources and, when appropriate, use a no-value simulation or small test rather than increasing exposure.

</details>
