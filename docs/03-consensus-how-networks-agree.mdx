# Consensus: How Networks Agree

:::info Time estimate
**Reading:** 15 min · **Videos:** 80 min · **Exercise/review:** 10 min · **Total:** about 105 min
:::

## Learning objectives

By the end, you should be able to explain the core mechanism in plain language, identify its main risks, and apply it to a realistic decision.

## Lesson

### The core idea

Consensus is the process by which independent nodes converge on accepted history despite delays, failures, and adversaries. It does not mean every participant agrees instantly or that every recorded claim is true; it means the network agrees that protocol-valid transactions were ordered into its ledger.

### How it works

Proof of work makes proposing history computationally expensive. Proof of stake requires validators to put economic value at risk and may punish provably bad behavior. Each design makes different assumptions about hardware, capital, governance, and recovery.

### Why it matters

Finality may be probabilistic, as confirmations make reversal less likely, or explicit after a validator threshold signs. A wallet's “pending” label represents this settlement process.

### Safety-first takeaway

Decentralization is multidimensional: node operation, validator concentration, client diversity, governance, infrastructure, token distribution, and bridge dependencies. Never reduce it to a single marketing number.

## Knowledge check

<details>
<summary>What should you understand before risking funds?</summary>

The transaction path, custody model, fees, failure modes, recovery procedure, and legal/tax recordkeeping—not merely a price prediction.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What is the safest response when you cannot explain a mechanism?</summary>

Pause. Research it with primary sources and, when appropriate, use a no-value simulation or small test rather than increasing exposure.

</details>
