# Practical Strategies and Risk Management

:::info Time estimate
**Reading:** 26 min · **Videos:** 3 min · **Exercise/review:** 10 min · **Total:** about 39 min
:::

## Learning objectives

By the end, you should be able to explain the core mechanism in plain language, identify its main risks, and apply it to a realistic decision.

## Lesson

### The core idea

Start with financial resilience: emergency fund, high-interest debt plan, insurance, retirement priorities, and a written limit for speculative assets. Crypto should not be the mechanism that makes an otherwise fragile plan more fragile.

### How it works

Dollar-cost averaging spreads entry timing but cannot turn a bad asset into a good one. Lump-sum exposure, rebalancing, and staged entries each have tradeoffs. Avoid leverage as a beginner; liquidation can turn a temporary move into permanent loss.

### Why it matters

Position sizing should start from loss capacity. If an asset can plausibly fall 80–100%, imagine that outcome in dollars and in effects on your life. Diversification across custody, counterparties, and recovery methods can be as important as asset count.

### Safety-first takeaway

Create a personal operating procedure: approved exchanges and wallets, transfer checklist, address book, MFA backup, tax logging, incident response, review cadence, and a trusted-person plan. Practice recovery with an empty or low-value wallet before it matters.

## Practice

1. Write a three-sentence explanation for a friend who has never used crypto.
2. List three ways a participant could lose money or access even if the network keeps running.
3. Identify one claim you would verify with a primary source before acting.

## Knowledge check

<details>
<summary>What should you understand before risking funds?</summary>

The transaction path, custody model, fees, failure modes, recovery procedure, and legal/tax recordkeeping—not merely a price prediction.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What is the safest response when you cannot explain a mechanism?</summary>

Pause. Research it with primary sources and, when appropriate, use a no-value simulation or small test rather than increasing exposure.

</details>
