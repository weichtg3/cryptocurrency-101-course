# How to Research a Crypto Project

:::info Time estimate
**Reading:** 28 min · **Videos:** 58 min · **Exercise/review:** 10 min · **Total:** about 96 min
:::

## Learning objectives

By the end, you should be able to explain the core mechanism in plain language, identify its main risks, and apply it to a realistic decision.

## Lesson

### The core idea

Research starts with a falsifiable thesis: the problem, the user, why a blockchain or token is required, and what evidence would prove the thesis wrong. Marketing partnerships and follower counts are not adoption evidence.

### How it works

Read primary materials: documentation, source repositories, block explorers, governance proposals, token contracts, security reports, and legal disclosures. Identify founders, funding, treasury, upgrade authority, multisignature signers, dependencies, and incident history.

### Why it matters

Verify activity rather than quoting one dashboard. Separate transactions from unique users, subsidized volume from organic use, locked value from borrowed or rehypothecated value, and developer commits from meaningful development.

### Safety-first takeaway

Use a red-flag screen: guaranteed returns, urgency, anonymous authority without compensating transparency, unauditable reserves, copied code, concentrated supply, hidden unlocks, unverifiable customers, paid promotion presented as research, or hostility to reasonable questions.

## Practice

1. Write a three-sentence explanation for a friend who has never used crypto.
2. List three ways a participant could lose money or access even if the network keeps running.
3. Identify one claim you would verify with a primary source before acting.

## Knowledge check

<details>
<summary>What should you understand before risking funds?</summary>

The transaction path, custody model, fees, failure modes, recovery procedure, and legal/tax recordkeeping—not merely a price prediction.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What is the safest response when you cannot explain a mechanism?</summary>

Pause. Research it with primary sources and, when appropriate, use a no-value simulation or small test rather than increasing exposure.

</details>
