## Lesson

### The core idea

Cryptocurrency is digitally represented value whose ownership and transfer are enforced by cryptography and a network's rules. It is not one product or one risk category. Bitcoin, stablecoins, smart-contract platforms, governance tokens, and collectibles solve different problems and fail in different ways.

### How it works

This lesson establishes the course vocabulary: asset, coin, token, wallet, exchange, network, address, transaction, block, confirmation, custody, and fiat. A coin is native to its network; a token is created on top of an existing platform. A wallet manages keys rather than holding coins in the everyday sense.

### Why it matters

The useful question is not “will crypto go up?” but “what system is this, what rights do I receive, who can change it, and how can I lose access?” Price is only one layer. Technical failure, fraud, operational mistakes, counterparty failure, and law all matter.

## Knowledge check

<details>
<summary>What should you understand before risking funds?</summary>

The transaction path, custody model, fees, failure modes, recovery procedure, and legal/tax recordkeeping—not merely a price prediction.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What is the safest response when you cannot explain a mechanism?</summary>

Pause. Research it with primary sources and, when appropriate, use a no-value simulation or small test rather than increasing exposure.

</details>
