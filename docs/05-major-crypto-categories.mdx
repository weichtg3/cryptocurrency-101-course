# Major Crypto Categories

:::info Time estimate
**Reading:** 18 min · **Videos:** 83 min · **Exercise/review:** 10 min · **Total:** about 111 min
:::

## Learning objectives

By the end, you should be able to explain the core mechanism in plain language, identify its main risks, and apply it to a realistic decision.

## Lesson

### The core idea

Native payment or store-of-value assets, smart-contract platforms, stablecoins, utility tokens, governance tokens, exchange tokens, wrapped assets, memecoins, and NFTs are not interchangeable. Classification helps expose the risk you are actually taking.

### How it works

Stablecoins may be backed by cash-like reserves, overcollateralized crypto, or algorithms. Ask who issues and redeems them, what backs them, where reserves sit, how attestations differ from audits, and what happens during stress.

### Why it matters

Layer 1 is a base network. Layer 2 processes activity using a base layer for some security or settlement. Bridges move representations across systems and introduce additional contracts, validators, or custodians.

### Safety-first takeaway

A token ticker is not an identity. Verify the network and contract address from authoritative sources. Copycat tokens can share a name and logo while being worthless or malicious.

3. Identify one claim you would verify with a primary source before acting.

## Knowledge check

<details>
<summary>What should you understand before risking funds?</summary>

The transaction path, custody model, fees, failure modes, recovery procedure, and legal/tax recordkeeping—not merely a price prediction.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What is the safest response when you cannot explain a mechanism?</summary>

Pause. Research it with primary sources and, when appropriate, use a no-value simulation or small test rather than increasing exposure.

</details>
