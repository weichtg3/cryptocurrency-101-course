# Exchanges and the Trading Landscape

## Learning objectives

By the end, you should be able to explain the core mechanism in plain language, identify its main risks, and apply it to a realistic decision.

## Lesson

### The core idea

A centralized exchange matches orders and may custody customer assets. A decentralized exchange uses contracts and liquidity mechanisms, but the user still depends on interfaces, tokens, oracles, bridges, and the correctness of code.

### How it works

A market order prioritizes execution; a limit order sets a price boundary but might not fill. The bid–ask spread, trading fee, slippage, withdrawal fee, and tax effect all contribute to total cost.

### Why it matters

Onboarding should include a unique password, phishing-resistant MFA when supported, withdrawal allowlists, anti-phishing codes, verified bookmarks, and review of account recovery. SMS is weaker than an authenticator or security key.

### Safety-first takeaway

Exchange balances create counterparty exposure. Self-custody creates key-management exposure. The right choice depends on purpose and competence; moving assets merely to follow a slogan can increase risk.

## Knowledge check

<details>
<summary>What should you understand before risking funds?</summary>

The transaction path, custody model, fees, failure modes, recovery procedure, and legal/tax recordkeeping—not merely a price prediction.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What is the safest response when you cannot explain a mechanism?</summary>

Pause. Research it with primary sources and, when appropriate, use a no-value simulation or small test rather than increasing exposure.

</details>
