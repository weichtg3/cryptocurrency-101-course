# NFTs and Web3: Use Cases vs Hype

:::info Time estimate
**Reading:** 18 min · **Videos:** 6 min · **Exercise/review:** 10 min · **Total:** about 34 min
:::

## Learning objectives

By the end, you should be able to explain the core mechanism in plain language, identify its main risks, and apply it to a realistic decision.

## Lesson

### The core idea

An NFT is a unique or distinguishable token record. It can point to media, represent membership, coordinate a game item, or serve as a receipt. The token and the referenced content are separate objects.

### How it works

Buying an NFT usually does not transfer copyright or unrestricted commercial rights. Read the license and platform terms. Metadata or media may be on-chain, content-addressed storage, or an ordinary server with different permanence.

### Why it matters

Useful evaluation asks what the token unlocks, who maintains the experience, whether the benefit needs a blockchain, how creators are paid, and what survives if the marketplace closes.

### Safety-first takeaway

Wash trading, spoofed collections, malicious approvals, fake mints, and illiquid pricing are common hazards. Treat rarity rankings and displayed floor prices as claims requiring verification.

## Knowledge check

<details>
<summary>What should you understand before risking funds?</summary>

The transaction path, custody model, fees, failure modes, recovery procedure, and legal/tax recordkeeping—not merely a price prediction.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What is the safest response when you cannot explain a mechanism?</summary>

Pause. Research it with primary sources and, when appropriate, use a no-value simulation or small test rather than increasing exposure.

</details>
