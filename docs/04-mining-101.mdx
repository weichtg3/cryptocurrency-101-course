# Mining 101

:::info Time estimate
**Reading:** 14 min · **Videos:** 77 min · **Exercise/review:** 10 min · **Total:** about 101 min
:::

## Learning objectives

By the end, you should be able to explain the core mechanism in plain language, identify its main risks, and apply it to a realistic decision.

## Lesson

### The core idea

Bitcoin miners assemble candidate blocks and repeatedly hash block headers, searching for a value below the network target. Finding it is difficult; verifying it is cheap. The protocol adjusts difficulty so blocks arrive near its target interval despite changing total hash power.

### How it works

Miner revenue combines newly issued bitcoin and transaction fees. The block subsidy periodically halves, so fees are expected to become more important over time. Revenue, electricity, hardware efficiency, uptime, and market price drive mining economics.

### Why it matters

Mining does not “solve useful equations” and it does not create arbitrary coins. It buys Sybil resistance: influence requires costly physical resources. Pools reduce income variance but can concentrate block construction.

### Safety-first takeaway

Cloud-mining promises deserve skepticism. A beginner should be able to explain hardware ownership, energy cost, payout rules, withdrawal conditions, and counterparty risk before treating a contract as anything other than a speculative claim.

## Knowledge check

<details>
<summary>What should you understand before risking funds?</summary>

The transaction path, custody model, fees, failure modes, recovery procedure, and legal/tax recordkeeping—not merely a price prediction.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What is the safest response when you cannot explain a mechanism?</summary>

Pause. Research it with primary sources and, when appropriate, use a no-value simulation or small test rather than increasing exposure.

</details>
