# Token Economics (Tokenomics)

:::info Time estimate
**Reading:** 22 min · **Videos:** 25 min · **Exercise/review:** 10 min · **Total:** about 57 min
:::

## Learning objectives

By the end, you should be able to explain the core mechanism in plain language, identify its main risks, and apply it to a realistic decision.

## Lesson

### The core idea

Tokenomics examines supply, distribution, issuance, incentives, utility, governance, and value capture. A low unit price does not make a token cheap: price multiplied by circulating supply gives market capitalization.

### How it works

Compare circulating, total, and maximum supply. Review vesting schedules, unlock cliffs, insider allocations, treasury control, inflation, staking emissions, and burns. Fully diluted valuation imagines the price applied to a broader supply, but it is a scenario rather than a promise.

### Why it matters

Demand for a product does not automatically create token demand. Ask whether users must acquire the token, fees are paid or burned in it, holders receive enforceable rights, or the token can be bypassed.

### Safety-first takeaway

Build a supply calendar. Large unlocks do not guarantee a crash, but they change who can sell. Treat “deflationary” as a mechanism to analyze, not a conclusion about price.

3. Identify one claim you would verify with a primary source before acting.

## Knowledge check

<details>
<summary>What should you understand before risking funds?</summary>

The transaction path, custody model, fees, failure modes, recovery procedure, and legal/tax recordkeeping—not merely a price prediction.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What is the safest response when you cannot explain a mechanism?</summary>

Pause. Research it with primary sources and, when appropriate, use a no-value simulation or small test rather than increasing exposure.

</details>
